# Simultaneous combat requirements

## Premise

Combat in traditional initiative-based turn order in D&D is tedious and slow. It also favors those who go later in the initiative order becasue they can change their intended actions based on how the battlefield has changed **since the beginning of the round**. This is an unfair advantage and obscures the chaos of battle. 

## Proposal

A combat encounter is still broken up into 6 second rounds. However there is no initiative roll. A combat round is split up into four discrete phases: planning, evaluation, reconciliation and resolution. These phases are described in more detail below.

### Phase 1: Planning

In this phase players are invited to prepare a sequence of orders that include the standard action, bonus action, reaction and movement mechanics that exist today in D&D. However the player must select order their intents across these possibilities in advance. A player might prepare the following round plan: prepare a reaction of Deflect missles, move to position B (15ft), attack the goblin, use a bonus action to disengage, then move to position C (15ft). 

The player submits that round plan. Once all actors have a submitted plan, the GM ends the planning phase and the plans are locked. 

### Phase 2. Evaluation

In this phase, the system steps through each plan, one planned activity at a time, in parallel across all plans. Based on the example above, the player prepares the reaction, and begins moving but moving takes time so other actors might be doing other things like drawing a bow. As the system continues evaluating the round the arrow flies, the reaction triggers, a contested roll takes place, and the outcome is recorded: critical hit on the player by a goblin weilding a bow. Damage is calculated to be 12 points which is enough to lower hte player's health to 0. Which brings us to reconciation.

### Phase 2a: Reconciliation

In this phase, the system examines the outcome fo the evaluation phase and determines what, if anything happens to the plans for the remainder of the round. In this case, the player was knocked unconscious so their plan can no longer be followed and is forfeit. The attack on the goblin never happens, and no more movemnent is possible. 

### Phase 3: Resolution

Once the actions taken in the round have been reconciled, the net result is presented through gameplay visualization.

#### Other considerations

- An unconscious player must make a death saving throw, which is thy only action then can plan during the planning phase.
- A player's whos next action has become invalidated, such as their attack target being destroyed, remains idle for the tick that that action would have been taken.
- In some cases reconciliation may be indeterminant and require GM intervention. This happens by presenting the GM with two proposed outcomes with the option to define a different outcome.
- The system must handle all kinds of contention: passing through the same space at the same time, players ending movement on the same space, random effects on a crit fail that target creatures that are already down, etc.
